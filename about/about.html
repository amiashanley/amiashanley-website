Amias Hanley

    ABOUT

    My practice explores how listening and signal-making are bound up with ongoing histories of surveillance, environmental politics, and representation. Through site-responsive and speculative sonic encounters, I examine how sound is instrumentalised, entangled, and contested, questioning who or what is being listened to, how meaning is assigned, and the power structures embedded in sonic relations. I work through these questions as spatialised composition, installation, sound sculpture, creative computing, writing, and performance.

    I am currently undertaking an AHRC-funded practice-based PhD at Queen Mary University of London (Geography and the Centre for Digital Music), supervised by Kathryn Yusoff and Anna Xambó Sedó, tracing covert biomimetic communication across the North Pacific and East Asian seas, with attention to relations among humans, machines and cetaceans.
